The First 72 HoursThe Family Emergency Handbook

The family emergency handbook for the people who can't just grab a bag: households with a baby, a grandparent, or someone who depends on medicines or powered care. A 139-page illustrated paperback, with a free 12-page Pocket Companion to keep on the fridge.

We will email you the Pocket Companion and tell you when the handbook launches. No more than a few messages a year; unsubscribe in one click. Privacy notice.

Who it is for

Written for the household that has to stay

Most emergency books are about the bag by the door. This one is about the fridge, the medicine cabinet, the cot and the oxygen concentrator. It works through the first three days of a power cut, a water failure, a flood or a storm for the people whose plan has to fit around someone else's needs.

01

Babies and infant feeding

How to keep infant feeds safe when the kettle, the steriliser and the fridge stop working. Clear stop lines on water, formula and hygiene, written to protect the smallest person in the house rather than to improvise around them.

02

Medicines and powered care

Insulin, cold-chain medicines, oxygen, home dialysis, powered beds and hoists. What runs out first, who to tell before the lights go out, and how to decide, calmly, when staying put is no longer the safe option.

03

Water, heat and sanitation

Counting the water you actually have, telling a boil notice from a chemical warning, heating one room without poisoning the household, and keeping toilets and hands safe when the taps run dry.

How we check our sources

Every claim traced, every source dated

Emergency advice is only useful if it is right. The handbook draws on around forty primary sources, mostly public-health and emergency-management bodies, and every safety-relevant statement in the text is tied to a numbered entry in a register.

What we do

  • Prefer primary guidance from public-health, water, food-safety and emergency-management authorities over secondary summaries.
  • Record the date each source was last checked, and re-check the register on a schedule rather than only at print time.
  • Keep hard stop lines, on water treatment, infant feeding, medicines and improvised shelter, exactly as the source states them.
  • Publish the full register online so it can be corrected faster than a printed book.

What we don't do

  • We don't give local telephone numbers, treatment times or agency names in the core text; those belong in clearly labelled regional editions.
  • We don't publish a safety claim we cannot trace to a source we have read ourselves.
  • We don't treat a confident forum post or product listing as evidence.
